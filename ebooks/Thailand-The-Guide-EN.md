## 1 · Introduction

### Why Thailand, now

There are few countries where, in a single day, you can wake to mist over northern rice terraces and the golden spires of a temple, eat the best meal of your life from a plastic stool, and fall asleep to a turquoise sea. Thailand is dazzling, warm, deeply hospitable — and remarkably easy to travel. Its infrastructure has matured (refurbished sleeper trains, cheap domestic flights, design hotels at gentle prices) while the country's soul — the smile, the *sanuk* (joy in all things), the street food — remains intact.

To travel Thailand now is to catch a moment of balance: a country developed enough to be comfortable, authentic enough to keep surprising you.

> **Did you know?** Thailand — formerly Siam — is the only country in South-East Asia never to have been colonised. A deep national pride that runs through its culture, architecture and outlook.

### Best seasons by type of trip

- **Beaches & islands:** November–April on the Andaman coast (Phuket, Krabi); February–April and July–August on the Gulf coast (Koh Samui, Koh Tao).
- **Culture & cities:** November–February (cool, dry, luminous).
- **Northern nature & mountains:** November–February; avoid March–April (crop-burning haze).
- **Festivals:** November (Loy Krathong & Yi Peng), mid-April (Songkran).
- **Low prices & lush nature:** June–October (monsoon, short showers, few crowds).

### A quick panorama: regions, distances, journey times

| Region | Heart | Best for | From Bangkok |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The North** | Chiang Mai, Pai, Mae Hong Son | Mountains, Lanna temples, road trips | Flight 1h15 · sleeper train 11–13h |
| **The Centre** | Bangkok, Ayutthaya, Kanchanaburi | City, history, rivers | — · Ayutthaya 1h30 by train |
| **Isan (NE)** | Khorat, Ubon, the Mekong | Khmer temples, silk, off-piste | Sleeper train 8–10h |
| **The South** | Phuket, Krabi, Khao Sok, the islands | Beaches, karsts, diving | Flight 1h15–1h30 |

> **Insider tip.** Don't try to "do" all four regions in one trip. Choose one or two and travel them slowly: Thailand rewards depth far more than distance.

### Local etiquette & faux pas to avoid

- **The head is sacred, the feet are low:** never touch anyone's head; never point your feet at a person or a Buddha image.
- **The *wai*:** return a palms-together greeting with a smile; no need to initiate it with service staff.
- **Saving face:** anger and a raised voice make everyone lose face — calm (*jai yen*, "a cool heart") resolves everything.
- **The monarchy** is protected by law (lèse-majesté): show absolute respect for the king and royal symbols.
- **At temples:** shoulders and knees covered, shoes off, voice low.

> **Avoid.** Raising your voice with a vendor or official; stepping over food set on the floor; (for women) touching a monk or handing him something directly.

## 3 · Getting there & getting around

### International access

- **Bangkok – Suvarnabhumi (BKK):** the main international hub; linked to the centre by the *Airport Rail Link*.
- **Bangkok – Don Mueang (DMK):** low-cost flights (AirAsia, Nok, Thai Lion).
- **Phuket (HKT), Chiang Mai (CNX), Krabi (KBV), Koh Samui (USM):** regional gateways (direct or via Bangkok).
- **Carriers:** Thai Airways, EVA Air, Qatar, Emirates, Turkish (via hubs); one-stop flights are common from Europe.

### Getting around

- **Domestic flights:** fast and cheap (AirAsia, Nok, Thai Lion, Bangkok Airways) — the winner for north↔south (1–1.5h vs 12h+ overland).
- **Trains (State Railway of Thailand):** scenic. Classes 1 (cabin), 2 (A/C, comfortable), 3 (basic). The **Bangkok–Chiang Mai sleeper** is a classic. Book ahead (counters, 12Go).
- **Buses & minivans:** reach everywhere; comfortable "VIP" coaches on big routes; minivans faster but cramped.
- **Ferries & speedboats:** link the islands (reduced in monsoon). **Long-tails** for short hops (agree the price first).
- **Scooters:** 200–300 THB/day; king of the North and the islands. **International Driving Permit required** + helmet (the law). See §11.
- **Taxi & ride-hailing:** **Grab** and **Bolt** (metered, in-app payment); metered taxis ("meter, please"); tuk-tuks (agree the fare first).
- **Car hire:** from ≈ 900–1,400 THB/day; international desks at airports; IDP + full insurance recommended.
- **Bicycles:** perfect in Ayutthaya, Sukhothai and old towns.

### Maps, passes & must-have apps

- **Grab / Bolt** (rides, food), **Google Maps** (offline areas), **Google Translate** (Thai pack + camera), **12Go** (trains/buses/ferries), **Klook** (tickets/tours), **LINE** (the local messenger), **XE** (currency).
- In Bangkok: a **Rabbit** card (BTS Skytrain) is handy for longer stays.

> **Insider tip.** For north↔south, fly; for Bangkok → Ayutthaya, take a cheap local train; the air-conditioned sleeper to Chiang Mai is worth it as an experience in itself.

### Optimised scenarios (fewer transfers)

- **Compact North:** Chiang Mai as a base, the Mae Hong Son loop, back; a single return flight.
- **No-backtrack South:** fly to Phuket or Krabi, then island-hop by ferry without returning to Bangkok.
- **Smart grand tour:** Bangkok → fly Chiang Mai → fly direct to the South (avoids re-crossing the country).

## 4 · Where to stay

### Recommended areas by profile

- **Bangkok:** *Old Town / Rattanakosin & riverside* (temples, relative calm, romantic); *Sukhumvit* (BTS, dining, nightlife, families); *Thonglor/Ekkamai* (hip); *Banglamphu/Khao San* (backpackers).
- **Chiang Mai:** *inside or just outside the moat* (temples on foot); *Nimmanhaemin* (cafés, design, younger crowd).
- **Krabi:** *Ao Nang* (access, amenities); *Railay* (at the foot of the cliffs, by boat only).
- **Islands:** west coast for sunsets; quiet beaches reachable by scooter.

Design hostels, characterful guesthouses, boutique hotels, resorts, private villas, **eco-lodges** and *raft-houses* (floating houses in national parks, e.g. Khao Sok), village homestays.

## 5 · Food & drink

### The must-eats, region by region

- **North:** *khao soi* (coconut-curry noodles, crisp noodles on top), *sai ua* (herby sausage), *nam prik num* (green-chilli dip), *gaeng hang lay* (Burmese-style pork curry).
- **Centre & Bangkok:** *pad krapow* (holy-basil stir-fry + fried egg), *tom yum goong* (hot-and-sour prawn soup), *pad thai*, *khao man gai* (chicken rice), *massaman*.
- **Isan:** *som tam* (green-papaya salad), *larb* (minced-meat salad), *gai yang* (grilled chicken), *khao niao* (sticky rice).
- **South:** richer, turmeric-gold curries, grilled seafood, *massaman*, sweet *roti*.
- **Desserts:** *khao niao mamuang* (mango sticky rice), banana *roti*.

> **Did you know?** Bangkok has **Michelin-starred street-food stalls** — pavement cooking here is an art form, not a last resort.

### At the table: etiquette, spice, hygiene, diets

- **Spice levels:** *"mai phet"* (not spicy), *"phet nit noi"* (a little — already a lot!).
- **Cutlery:** spoon (right hand) + fork (to push); chopsticks for noodles. Dishes are shared in the centre.
- **Hygiene:** eat where the local queue is long, cooked fresh and hot. **Tap water isn't drinkable** (bottled/filtered); café ice is generally safe.
- **Vegetarian/vegan:** *"mangsawirat"* (veg), *"jay"* (strict vegan — yellow-red *เจ* flag); watch for hidden fish sauce & shrimp paste (*"mai sai nam pla / kapi"*).
- **Halal:** a Muslim presence in the South and Bangkok (halal restaurants are signed); **kosher** and **gluten-free**: carry an allergen translation card.

### Drinks, cafés, bars, rooftops

*Cha yen* (sweet orange iced tea), *nam manao* (fresh lime soda), fruit shakes, ice-cold **Singha/Chang/Leo** beers, roadside coffee (Café Amazon everywhere) and a strong specialty scene in Bangkok and Chiang Mai. **Rooftops** in Bangkok for the skyline (smart-casual dress).

### Food experiences

**Cooking classes** (half-day, market + wok — a highlight), nighttime **food tours** (Chinatown, markets).

### A few reference tables *(iconic · tier · revalidate)*

- **Bangkok:** *Thipsamai* (legendary pad thai, Old Town, budget) · *Err* (rustic-urban Thai, mid) · *Jay Fai* (Michelin-starred street food, Old Town — luxury by price).
- **Chiang Mai:** night markets & *khao soi* shophouses (budget) · northern kitchens (mid).
- **South:** beachside seafood by weight (mid) · resort fine dining (luxury).

## 6 · Culture, history & the arts

### A synthetic timeline

- **Early kingdoms:** Dvaravati, **Khmer** influence (Isan temples, 10th–12th c.).
- **Sukhothai (13th c.):** first Thai kingdom, golden age of art and script.
- **Ayutthaya (1351–1767):** a powerful, cosmopolitan capital, destroyed by the Burmese.
- **Rattanakosin / Bangkok (from 1782):** the Chakri dynasty; Siam never colonised.
- **20th–21st c.:** modernisation, constitutional monarchy, tourism and a creative boom.

### Heritage & major sites

Bangkok's *wats* (Wat Pho, Wat Arun, Wat Phra Kaew), the ruins of **Ayutthaya** and **Sukhothai** (UNESCO), Chiang Mai's Lanna temples (Doi Suthep), Isan's Khmer sanctuaries (Phimai, Phanom Rung). Museums: Bangkok National Museum, *Jim Thompson House* (silk & art).

### Contemporary scenes

Design and *creative districts* (Charoenkrung in Bangkok), photography, auteur cinema (Apichatpong Weerasethakul, Palme d'Or), indie music, living craft (ceramics, weaving, lacquer).

### Key festivals *(dates to revalidate each year)*

| Festival | When | Where | Book |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Songkran** (new year water fights) | 13–15 Apr | Nationwide (Chiang Mai intense) | Hotels very early |
| **Loy Krathong / Yi Peng** (lanterns) | Nov (full moon) | Chiang Mai, Sukhothai | Hotels & lantern tickets |
| **Vegetarian Festival** | Oct | Phuket, Bangkok | — |
| **Flower Festival** | Feb | Chiang Mai | — |

> **Insider tip.** For Yi Peng in Chiang Mai, book accommodation months ahead; be wary of commercial "lantern releases" and favour respectful ceremonies.

## 7 · Things to do

- **Classics:** temples & palaces, viewpoints (Doi Suthep), beaches, floating & night markets.
- **Nature & adventure:** treks and hill-tribe villages (North), **national parks** (Khao Sok, Khao Yai, Doi Inthanon — *official source: DNP, dnp.go.th*), diving/snorkelling (Koh Tao, Similan — seasonal), caves (Tham Lod), kayaking the karsts (Phang Nga, Krabi), ziplines.
- **Elephants — ethically:** only **genuine sanctuaries** where you do **not ride** the animals (observation, care). See §12.
- **Wellness:** Thai massage (from 250 THB/h), spas, retreats (*vipassana* meditation) — etiquette: modest dress, quiet.
- **Families:** parks, aquariums, educational farms, calm beaches, kid-friendly cooking classes.
- **Nightlife:** shows, night markets, party districts — stay alert (drinks, two-wheelers).
- **Photography:** Wat Arun at dawn (west bank, ~13.7437, 100.4889); Pai's sea of mist at sunrise; Railay at sunset; Doi Inthanon early. *(Approximate coordinates.)*

> **Avoid.** Any "selfie" with drugged wild animals; operators who promise elephant or tiger "rides."

## 8 · Ready-to-use itineraries

*(Each day: morning / afternoon / evening, indicative travel time, estimated budget/person.)*

### A. 3 days — Bangkok, city rhythm
- **D1:** *Morning* Grand Palace & Wat Phra Kaew → Wat Pho (Reclining Buddha). *Afternoon* canal (*khlong*) boat + Wat Arun. *Evening* Chinatown (Yaowarat) street food. *Budget* ≈ 1,500–3,000 THB.
- **D2:** *Morning* Chatuchak market (weekend) or a museum. *Afternoon* Jim Thompson House. *Evening* rooftop. ≈ 1,500–3,500 THB.
- **D3:** Day trip to **Ayutthaya** (train, cycle the ruins), back by evening. ≈ 1,200–2,500 THB.

### B. 7 days — The essentials (city + nature)
D1–3 Bangkok (above) → **fly Chiang Mai** → D4–5 Chiang Mai (temples, cooking class, ethical elephant sanctuary) → D6 Pai or Doi Suthep → D7 return. *Ideal first trip.*

### C. 10–14 days — Grand tour (multi-region)
D1–3 Bangkok + Ayutthaya → fly **Chiang Mai** D4–7 (northern loop, elephants) → fly **South (Krabi)** D8–10 → islands & karsts D11–13 → D14 return. *Two internal flights, little backtracking.*

### D. 10 days — Off the beaten track
Bangkok → **Isan** (Khao Yai, Phimai, Phanom Rung, the Mekong at Nong Khai) → return. Khmer temples, silk villages, warm welcomes, low prices.

### Variants
- **Family:** gentler pace, calm beaches (Koh Lanta), parks, half-days.
- **Backpacker:** sleeper trains + buses, hostels, North loop + Koh Tao (cheap diving).

> **Insider tip.** Slip **one empty day** into each leg: the unplanned afternoons often become the best memories.

## 9 · Shopping & craft

- **Where:** markets (Chatuchak in Bangkok, Chiang Mai's night bazaars), air-conditioned malls (ICONSIAM), village workshops (Isan silk, Lampang ceramics, Bo Sang umbrellas).
- **What's worth buying:** Thai silk, celadon, lacquerware, teak objects, curry pastes & spices, cotton clothing, hill-tribe craft.
- **Counterfeits:** everywhere (brands, watches) — illegal to import into many countries; favour authentic craft.
- **Export rules:** it is **forbidden** to export genuine **antiques** and old **Buddha images** without permission (Fine Arts Department); restrictions on some food/animal products (CITES). *(Revalidate before buying.)*

> **Avoid.** Buying an old Buddha head as a "souvenir": exporting it is forbidden — and the gesture is disrespectful.

## 11 · Practical & safety

### Health
Pharmacies are excellent and cheap (minor advice); good private hospitals in Bangkok, Phuket, Chiang Mai. **Minimum kit:** rehydration salts, anti-diarrhoeals, antiseptic, plasters, repellent (DEET), sunscreen, personal medicines. **Risks:** sun/dehydration, mosquitoes (dengue — no traveller vaccine, so protect yourself), jellyfish (seasonal/by beach — heed flags), coral cuts.

### Safety
Generally **very safe**; petty theft is the main risk. **Common scams:**
- "The temple/palace is closed today" (false; the tout wants to take you to a gem shop).
- Dirt-cheap tuk-tuk "tours" (commission detours).
- Rigged taxi meters (insist on the meter).
- "Damage" claims on jet-skis/scooters (photograph everything at pickup).
- Over-friendly strangers, card games, "free" drinks.

**Night travel:** avoid riding a two-wheeler at night in rural areas.

### Travel insurance — what to check
High **medical & repatriation** cover; **motorbike riding** explicitly included (often excluded by default, and you need valid motorcycle + international licences); sports/diving if relevant; theft/baggage; a 24/7 assistance line.

### Rights & emergencies
Police **191** · Ambulance **1669** · **Tourist Police (English) 1155** · TAT **1672**.

> **Did you know?** The **Tourist Police (1155)** speak English and exist specifically for travellers — a useful reflex in any dispute or loss.

## 12 · Responsible & ethical travel

- **Animal sanctuaries:** choose refuges where elephants are **neither ridden nor trained for shows**; beware the over-used word "sanctuary" — check practices and reviews. No tiger selfies.
- **Responsible operators:** favour local guides, village cooperatives, recognised labels.
- **Cultural impact:** ask before photographing people (especially hill-tribe elders); dress and behave modestly at sacred sites; a small donation maintains the temples.
- **Plastic, water, wildlife:** carry a reusable bottle (plastic waste is a real local problem); **reef-safe sunscreen**; never touch coral or feed wildlife; carry out your litter.

> **Insider tip.** Spend "local": markets, family kitchens, artisans, local guides — that's where your money does the most good.

## 13 · For specific travellers

- **Solo women:** widely regarded as safe and easy; usual night-time common sense; modest dress appreciated away from the beach; reliable night transport (trains, Grab).
- **Accessibility (reduced mobility):** Bangkok is improving (BTS lifts at some stations, accessible big hotels) but pavements are uneven and temples often have steps; favour newer hotels, check accessibility in advance, plan private taxis/transfers; some beaches/resorts offer adapted access. *(Revalidate site by site.)*
- **LGBTQ+:** among Asia's most welcoming destinations; visible scenes in Bangkok, Phuket, Chiang Mai; usual discretion in rural/religious areas.
- **Digital nomads:** fast, cheap internet; many coworking spaces and cafés (Chiang Mai and Bangkok lead); check the latest **long-stay / remote-work visas** (*revalidate with immigration*).
- **Business travellers:** Bangkok's business districts (Sathorn, Sukhumvit, Silom); hotels with meeting rooms; courteous protocol, exchange business cards with both hands; a small **corporate gift** is appreciated (avoid the overly personal).
